## Statement fidelity (arXiv:2610.08144)

Kernel acceptance certifies the formal proposition and the formal derivation
supplied for it — not that the proposition is the one the source text states,
that the derivation follows the source argument, or that the source argument
is correct. Compilation is never the acceptance criterion for "the source
proof is correct."

When the task is to formalize an *existing* argument (as opposed to proving a
statement by any means), answer three modes explicitly:

- **M1 — Statement correspondence.** Does the formalized theorem state what
  the source states? Compare quantifiers, side conditions, exponents,
  domains, norms, dependencies, implicit existence conditions. Record any
  weakening, strengthening, or change.
- **M2 — Argument correspondence.** Does the formal derivation follow the
  source's argument, or prove the same proposition by a different route (or
  repair a broken one)? Label it: *faithful formalization of the source
  proof* vs *independent proof of the formalized statement*.
- **M3 — Credit honesty.** Does the claimed verification credit match what is
  actually verified? Disclose sorrys, axioms, divergences, scope limits.
