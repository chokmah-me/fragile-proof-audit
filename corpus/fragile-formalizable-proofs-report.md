# Fragile but Formalizable: A Scout Report on Lean 4–Attackable Proofs (1700–2026)

**TL;DR.** The hunt turned up **four live targets** where a weeks-scale Lean 4 effort could plausibly confirm, refute, or pinpoint the broken step: (1) a **January 2026 preprint claiming the irrationality of every odd zeta value**, whose entire proof funnels into one decay estimate that can be tested numerically in an afternoon; (2) a 2024 **"complete congruence system" claim for the Erdős–Straus conjecture**, reducible to a finite check on residue classes mod 840; (3) the recurring genre of **Agoh–Giuga primality-characterization claims**, which must survive a finite audit against the seven known Giuga numbers; and (4) a 2026 **interval-certificate claim that the de Bruijn–Newman constant is at most 0.1787854**, which is less a fragile proof than a live, machine-checkable audit target. One candidate — Shekhar Suman's 2024 claimed proof that ζ(5) is irrational — was **already refuted in January 2025 by exactly the gate mechanism this report advocates**, and serves as the proof-of-concept kill. On the historical side, Lamé's 1847 FLT "proof," Kempe's 1879 four-color "proof," and Tait's 1884 conjecture all reduce to **pinpointable broken steps** (a class-number-3 failure, a nine-vertex Kempe-chain tangle, a 46-vertex graph) that are ideal weeks-scale Lean formalizations. The mega-claims — Joshi's abc, de Branges' RH, Zhang's Landau–Siegel zeros — are **not** weeks-scale targets: their decisive steps are either analytic inequality towers or machinery with no mathlib substrate.

---

## 1. Calibration: What "Weeks, Not Years" Actually Means in 2026

Before scoring candidates, we need an honest yardstick for what a small Lean 4 + mathlib team (or a solo expert with modern tooling) can close in weeks. The record is now substantial and, as of this month, moving fast.

The cleanest data point is the **Polynomial Freiman–Ruzsa conjecture**. Gowers, Green, Manners, and Tao posted the proof in November 2023; a crowdsourced Lean 4 project organized around a human-readable "blueprint" produced a complete, `sorry`-free formalization **in three weeks**, finishing before the paper was even submitted for publication ([arXiv:2311.05762](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.05762), [Lean Zulip archive](https://leanprover-community.github.io/archive/stream/412902-Polynomial-Freiman-Ruzsa-conjecture/topic/Project.20completed!.20.20Thoughts.20and.20reflections.3F.html)). The proof's spine — Shannon entropy inequalities plus elementary finite-group combinatorics — is precisely the profile this report scores as "formalizable." A participant's post-mortem is blunt about the boundary conditions: real-time formalization works when a blueprint exists and the mathematics is algebraic/combinatorial; a Fargues–Scholze-scale geometric edifice would still not close "in three weeks (or even one year and a half)" ([Lean Zulip archive](https://leanprover-community.github.io/archive/stream/412902-Polynomial-Freiman-Ruzsa-conjecture/topic/Project.20completed!.20.20Thoughts.20and.20reflections.3F.html)).

The second calibration point is the genre of **certificate-based finite proofs**. The Boolean Pythagorean Triples problem was settled in 2016 by a 200-terabyte SAT proof (compressed certificate: 68 GB) showing that {1,…,7824} admits a triple-free 2-coloring while {1,…,7825} does not ([arXiv:1605.00723](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.00723), [CACM](https://cacm.acm.org/research/the-science-of-brute-force/)). Cruz-Filipe and Schneider-Kamp then closed the loop in Coq, formally proving that the propositional encoding *means* what the mathematicians claimed it means ([Cruz-Filipe & Schneider-Kamp](http://imada.sdu.dk/~lcf/pubs/paper37.pdf)). Keller's 90-year-old cube-tiling conjecture went the same way: Brakensiek, Heule, Mackey, and Narváez resolved the last open dimension (d = 7, answer: true) with ~200 GB of SAT proofs in 2020 ([Quanta](https://www.quantamagazine.org/computer-search-settles-90-year-old-math-problem-20200819/), [CMU project page](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~mheule/Keller/)); the reduction to clique-finding in generalized Keller graphs was formalized in Lean 3 ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3573105.3575669)), and an **end-to-end Lean 4 verification of Keller's conjecture for all dimensions** appeared at ITP 2026 ([DROPS/LIPIcs](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITP.2026.26)). These are the existence proofs for the report's central thesis: proofs whose load-bearing content is a *finite computation plus a reduction* are now formally closable, and the certificate layer is the natural "gate."

The third calibration point is the speed frontier. Apéry's irrationality of ζ(3) — via Beukers' integrals — was fully formalized in **Lean 4 in early 2025** by Liu, Zhang, and Zhi, including the needed asymptotics of lcm(1,…,n) from the Prime Number Theorem project ([arXiv:2503.07625](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07625v1)); the same proof had previously been done in Isabelle (Eberl, 2019) and in Coq with computer-algebra certificates (Mahboubi–Sibut-Pinote, 2021) ([arXiv:2503.07625](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07625v1)). Ellenberg–Gijswijt's cap-set breakthrough was formalized in Lean by Dahmen, Hölzl, and Lewis in weeks-scale effort in 2019, with the final asymptotic constant computed inside the proof assistant itself ([ITP 2019](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITP.2019.15)). And at the extreme end: in September 2026, Anthropic reported that Claude produced a complete, kernel-checked Lean proof of **Fermat's Last Theorem** in eleven days of largely autonomous work — 13 million lines of Lean, 29,500 intermediate theorems — a milestone Buzzard's EPSRC-funded human project had budgeted five years for ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formalizing-fermats-last-theorem), [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/claude/articles/claude-formalized-fermats-last-theorem-133556159.html)). Whether or not that artifact ages well, the direction is unambiguous: the cost of *testing* a formalizable proof is collapsing.

**Summary of the yardstick.** A proof is a "weeks-scale" target when (a) its spine is elementary algebra, finite combinatorics, or explicit estimates over mathlib-covered territory; (b) it can be blueprinted into independent lemmas; and (c) the decisive residue is a finite check or a single displayed estimate. Everything in Tier 1 below meets all three. The mega-claims meet none.

## 2. The Scoring Rubric

Each candidate is scored 0–10 on the three axes from the brief, with the interpretation made operational:

| Axis | What 10 looks like | What 0 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Formalizability** | Determinants, recurrences, finite sums, congruences, explicit constants; mathlib already has the vocabulary (Legendre polynomials, cyclotomic rings, SAT encodings, graph colorings) | Stacks, anabelian geometry, ad-hoc infinite-dimensional operator theory; even the *statement* resists formalization |
| **Fragility signature** | Unpublished sweeping claim + sketch prose at the decisive step + constants folded into constants + single inequality chain bearing all weight + author outside specialty | Refereed, replicated, certificates published, multiple independent proofs |
| **Refutability** | A quantity computable at small parameters whose sign/parity/integrality decides the proof route (e.g., "Eq. (48) has integer solutions at n = 1") | No finite consequence; failure modes only detectable by experts reading prose |

Two rules of engagement. First, **the gate refutes the route, not necessarily the theorem**: ζ(5) may well be irrational even though every claimed proof to date fails; what a gate kills is the specific chain of lemmas. Second, the historical cases (Lamé, Kempe, Tait) score high on "fragility" retroactively — the fragility is proven fact, and the exercise there is *pinpointing* the broken step formally, which is valuable both pedagogically and as infrastructure for evaluating future claims in the same genre.

## 3. Tier 1: Live Targets Worth Attacking Now

### 3.1 The January 2026 "all odd zeta values" preprint — the single best target in the harvest

**The claim.** A preprint posted 21 January 2026, *On the Irrationality of the Odd Zeta Values*, claims to prove that **ζ(2n+1) is irrational for every n ≥ 1** — in one stroke subsuming Apéry's ζ(3) and settling ζ(5), ζ(7), …, a wall at which the field has been stuck for four decades ([Preprints.org 202601.1609](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202601.1609)). For context: the state of the art is Ball–Rivoal's theorem that the ℚ-span of {1, ζ(3), ζ(5), …} is infinite-dimensional, and Zudilin's theorem that at least one of ζ(5), ζ(7), ζ(9), ζ(11) is irrational — *no specific* odd value beyond ζ(3) is known irrational ([IHÉS survey slides](https://www.ihes.fr/~brown/IrrationalitySlidesPrintable.pdf), [Quanta](https://www.quantamagazine.org/rational-or-not-this-basic-math-question-took-decades-to-answer-20250108/)).

**The load-bearing chain.** The preprint constructs explicit integer linear forms Λ_m^(q) = A_m^(q)·ζ(2n+1) − B_m^(q) and invokes the standard irrationality criterion: if 0 < |A_m x − B_m| ≤ ε_m → 0 with A_m ≠ 0, then x is irrational. Everything therefore rests on two analytic claims: (i) the forms are nonzero, and (ii) ‖Λ_m^(q)‖ → 0, which the paper reduces to a parameter analysis of a function g(α) with a stationary point α* = q/(e^λ − 1) and a sign condition g(α*) < 0 ([Preprints.org 202601.1609](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202601.1609)).

**The Lean-formalizable slice.** The irrationality criterion itself is a ten-line formal theorem (it is the same criterion whose Lean 4 instantiation closed Apéry's theorem in 2025 ([arXiv:2503.07625](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07625v1))). The algebraic scaffolding — the claimed construction of A_m, B_m as integers, the recurrence/denominator bookkeeping — is elementary and blueprintable. What remains isolated is exactly the two gates above: nonvanishing and decay.

**The numerically testable step.** Compute the paper's Λ_m^(q) for, say, ζ(5), at m = 1…50 across its parameter range, in exact rational-plus-interval arithmetic. The gate values: the proof needs 0 < ‖Λ_m^(q)‖ → 0 with explicit decay beating the denominator growth (the Apéry-shaped inequality, cf. §3.2's e³/24 < 1 computation). **Confirm:** a displayed, verified decay ‖Λ_m‖ ≤ ρ^m with ρ·e^{denominator exponent} < 1 — that would be a genuine breakthrough worth escalating to experts. **Break:** ‖Λ_m‖ bounded away from 0, or Λ_m ≡ 0 identically (a hidden exact relation), or the claimed roots α₁ < α₂ of g(α) = 0 failing to exist at the stated parameters. Given that an eleven-author team found the fatal flaw in the last ζ(5) claim within months ([arXiv:2411.16774](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16774v3)), and given the fragility profile here — sweeping claim, criterion invocation substituting for a displayed decay computation — the prior is strongly on "break."

**Verdict: attack.** Estimated effort: 1–2 weeks for the numerical gate; 3–6 weeks for a Lean slice formalizing the criterion plus the linear-form construction, leaving the decay estimate as an isolated, testable `sorry`-gate. The outcome is valuable either way: a refutation closes a public claim; a surviving gate would be genuinely newsworthy.

### 3.2 Suman's ζ(5) claim (2024) — the completed demonstration of the method

**The claim and its fate.** In 2024, Shekhar Suman posted *A note on the irrationality of ζ(5) and higher odd zeta values* (arXiv:2407.07121), claiming irrationality of ζ(5) via a Beukers-style integral I_n and an induction on a Diophantine equation. In January 2025, Chen and ten collaborators published *A Note on a Recent Attempt to Prove the Irrationality of ζ(5)*, pinpointing the failure: the proof needs Eq. (47)/(48) to have **no** integer solutions, but at the induction base n = 1 the equation forces a − 2b ∈ {0, −b}, i.e., a = 2b or a = b — valid integer solutions that Suman dismissed only because they would make ζ(5) equal to 2 or 1, "absurd" since ζ(5) is not an integer — a circular appeal, since the solvability of the equation is logically independent of ζ(5)'s arithmetic ([arXiv:2411.16774](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16774v3), [QEIOS mirror](https://www.qeios.com/read/NAI617)).

**Why this is the proof of concept.** The refutation is exactly a gate of the type this report hunts: a small-parameter computation (n = 1) producing a positive result (solutions exist) where the proof needed a negative one. I reproduced the trivial arithmetic: ζ(5) = 1.0369277551…, so a = b ⇒ ζ(5) = 1 is off by 0.037 — the contradiction Suman needed simply isn't there, and the induction never starts. The rebuttal note adds a second, quantitative gate: numerical verification that Suman's I_n **fails the decay condition** of the standard criterion — the linear forms do not tend to zero fast enough to force irrationality ([arXiv:2411.16774](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16774v3)).

**The gate arithmetic, displayed.** Beukers' successful ζ(3) argument balances a per-step decay of 1/24^n against denominator growth d_n³ ~ e^{3n}, and closes because e³/24 = 0.8368… < 1 (my computation, matching the formalized bound J_n ≤ 2·(1/24)^n·ζ(3) in the Lean 4 proof ([arXiv:2503.07625](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07625v1))). For ζ(5), denominators grow like e^{5n}, so a linear form must decay faster than e^{−5} ≈ 0.00674 per step; no one has constructed forms beating ρ ≈ 1/25, and 25 < e⁵ ≈ 148.4, so the inequality fails by a factor of ~6. This is why every ζ(5) claim built on "an integral of Beukers type" is fragile *by construction*: the genre's gate is a single number, ρ·e⁵, and it keeps coming out greater than 1.

**Verdict: already killed — use as the training exercise.** A Lean artifact reproducing both gates (the n = 1 solvability of Eq. (48), and the failure of the decay condition at small n) would be a permanent, citable "refutation object," and the warm-up for §3.1. Effort: days.

### 3.3 Claimed "complete congruence systems" for the Erdős–Straus conjecture (2024)

**The claim.** The Erdős–Straus conjecture (1948) asserts that for every n ≥ 2, 4/n = 1/x + 1/y + 1/z has solutions in positive integers. It is verified computationally through n ≤ 10¹⁷ (Salez, 2014) with a 2025 report of extension to 10¹⁸, and classical reductions (Mordell) show it suffices to treat primes n ≡ r (mod 840) for r ∈ {1², 11², 13², 17², 19², 23²} = {1, 121, 169, 289, 361, 529} ([arXiv:2101.00975](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.00975v1), [ProofAtlas](https://proofatlas.ai/collaboration/erdos-straus-conjecture/)). In April 2024 a preprint claimed *A Complete Congruence System for the Erdős–Straus Conjecture* — i.e., a finite set of covering identities purportedly handling every residue class ([arXiv:2404.01508](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01508v3)). A 2025 preprint likewise claims "almost a complete proof" of the generalized 5/n case ([arXiv:2508.07367](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07367v1)).

**The load-bearing chain.** These arguments are pure elementary number theory: divisibility parametrizations (a/b is a sum of two unit fractions iff there exist divisors u, v of b with a | u+v), case splits by residue class, and a claimed *covering* — every prime in the hard classes mod 840 lands in at least one parametrized family. The decisive step is invariably the covering claim at the hard residues; the 2024 preprint's appendix, for instance, reduces everything to "4d−1 | u+v for divisors u,v of k+d" and asserts the odd-k case is automatic ([arXiv:2404.01508](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01508v3)).

**Lean slice and numeric gate.** The entire vocabulary — divisors, congruences, Egyptian fraction decompositions — is mathlib-native. The gate is a finite sweep: enumerate the first ~10⁴ primes in each hard class (p ≡ 121, 169, 289, 361, 529, 1 mod 840), and for each, test whether the paper's parametrized families actually produce a decomposition. **Confirm:** every tested prime covered with the claimed parameters (then escalate — a correct complete system would be a theorem). **Break:** a single uncovered prime, or parameters violating the stated divisibility (e.g., u ∤ k+d), kills the covering lemma. Because the check is per-prime and exact, the first failure is a publishable pinpoint, not a heuristic doubt. Historical base rate demands this gate: the conjecture has absorbed claimed proofs for 75 years while finite verification keeps passing (10¹⁸ and counting) ([ProofAtlas](https://proofatlas.ai/collaboration/erdos-straus-conjecture/)).

**Verdict: attack.** 2–4 weeks, most of it writing the divisor-parametrization library cleanly in Lean; the gate itself runs overnight. High expected yield because the claim's decisive step is *finite and displayed* — rare among open-problem claims.

### 3.4 Agoh–Giuga primality characterizations — a genre with a built-in counterexample oracle

**The claim (genre).** Giuga's 1950 conjecture: n is prime iff Σ_{a=1}^{n−1} a^{n−1} ≡ −1 (mod n); equivalently (Agoh's formulation), a Bernoulli-number congruence characterizes primality ([ProofAtlas](https://proofatlas.ai/collaboration/agoh-giuga-conjecture/), [CARMA survey](https://carmamaths.org/jon/giuga2013.pdf)). Claimed proofs surface regularly; the conjecture remains open. A composite satisfying the congruence must be simultaneously a **Giuga number** (p | n/p − 1 for all p | n) and a **Carmichael number** ((p−1) | (n−1) for all p | n); only seven Giuga numbers are known, and any counterexample must exceed 10^19908 — 19,908 decimal digits, with at least 4,771 distinct prime factors ([ProofAtlas](https://proofatlas.ai/collaboration/agoh-giuga-conjecture/), [CARMA survey](https://carmamaths.org/jon/giuga2013.pdf)).

**Load-bearing chain and Lean slice.** Every attempted proof reduces to Korselt-style criteria and congruence bookkeeping — squarefree analysis, p-adic divisibility chains, Bernoulli denominators (von Staudt–Clausen). This is among the most mathlib-ready territory in all of number theory (Bernoulli numbers, Carmichael/Korselt, finite field units are all present). A claimed proof can be blueprinted lemma-by-lemma; experience says the break surfaces as an unjustified divisibility assertion in the composite case.

**The numeric gate (two-sided).** *Against a claimed proof:* any correct proof must imply, for each known Giuga number g, that g fails Korselt's criterion — checkable exactly: I verified 30, 858, 1722, 66198, 2214408306, and 24423128562 are all Giuga and all non-Carmichael (e.g., for 30: 2|14, 3|9, 5|5 ✓, but 29 ≢ 0 mod 2,4 ✗). If a claimed proof's lemmas, specialized to g = 30, "prove" a false divisibility, the lemma is located and false. *For the conjecture's truth:* the counterexample search is a pure computation bounded below at 10^19908 — no gate will close truth, but the bound itself (a sieve certificate) is a formalization target of independent value.

**Verdict: attack, as a genre.** Each new claimed proof is a 1–2 week audit; the reusable infrastructure (Giuga/Korselt/Agoh library in Lean) is a one-time 3–4 week investment that pays off per claim.

### 3.5 Gomila's Λ ≤ 0.1787854 (2026) — a different species: the live certificate audit

**The claim.** In August 2026, Jude Gomila announced a computer-assisted proof lowering the de Bruijn–Newman constant's ceiling from 0.2 (Platt–Trudgian, 2020) to exactly 129/800 + 87677/5,000,000 = **0.1787854**, instantiating Polymath 15's Theorem 1.2 at an exact rational parameter row, supported by ~3.15 million machine-checked interval certificates plus an 883-prism barrier argument ([Gomila](https://www.judegomila.com/posts/riemann-lambda-0.1787854)). Recall the stakes: RH is equivalent to Λ ≤ 0, and Rodgers–Tao proved Λ ≥ 0 in 2018, so the truth is confined to [0, 0.1787854] ([Polymath 15 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.12438), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Bruijn%E2%80%93Newman_constant), [Gomila](https://www.judegomila.com/posts/riemann-lambda-0.1787854)). The claim carries unusual provenance for the genre: Dan Romik is named as independent reviewer, and the audit trail is hash-pinned ([Gomila](https://www.judegomila.com/posts/riemann-lambda-0.1787854)).

**Why it belongs in this report.** This is the *inverse* of a fragile proof: the claim is explicitly structured as three finite, independently checkable gates — (i) RH verified to height X/2 (consumed from Platt–Trudgian), (ii) a final-time zero-free scan over 3,149,013 windows, (iii) a barrier zero-free at all intermediate times — feeding a proved criterion ([Gomila](https://www.judegomila.com/posts/riemann-lambda-0.1787854)). Formalizability is medium (the barrier and tail lemmas need real analytic estimates, but interval arithmetic in Lean is established practice, as in Helfgott–Platt's Goldbach verification up to 8.875·10³⁰ ([Helfgott–Platt via arXiv:1404.2224](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.2224))). Refutability is maximal: replay the certificates; if any window's interval contains a sign change the criterion forbids, the bound collapses to 0.2.

**Verdict: attack, as verification rather than refutation.** The deliverable is a Lean-checked replay of a *sampled stratum* of certificates plus formal statements of the three gate lemmas. 3–6 weeks. Whichever way it lands, the field gets a cleaner Λ.

## 4. Tier 2: Historical Broken Steps — Pinpoint Jobs

These are the "dredged" cases: proofs (or proof-shaped objects) whose failure is historically known but whose *exact* broken step has never been pinned by a proof assistant. Each is a self-contained weeks-scale Lean project with a permanent payoff — a formally certified "the error is here" artifact, and reusable infrastructure for evaluating modern claims in the same genre.

### 4.1 Lamé's 1847 FLT "proof": the class-number gate

**The claim and the break.** On 4 January 1847, Lamé proposed proving FLT by factoring xⁿ + yⁿ over the cyclotomic integers ℤ[ζₙ]; the argument requires unique factorization of cyclotomic integers, which Kummer had already (1844, published; known to Kummer by 1843) shown fails — the first failure occurs at n = 23, where the class number is 3 ([Lund University](https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/publication/9054900)). Kummer salvaged the strategy for *regular* primes via ideal numbers.

**The Lean slice and gate.** mathlib has cyclotomic fields, rings of integers, class groups, and — decisively — a **completed formalization of FLT for regular primes** (the flt-regular project), which already carries Kummer's criterion infrastructure ([AFM/Episciences](https://afm.episciences.org/16046/pdf)). The pinpoint artifact: a Lean theorem exhibiting the failure of unique factorization in ℤ[ζ₂₃] concretely — e.g., a verified counterexample to the "prime ⇒ irreducible" implication or a computation that the class group has order 3 — placed at the exact line of Lamé's argument where irreducibility of the factors x + ζʲy is invoked. **Gate values:** class number h₂₃ = 3 (confirm the break); any formalization path that *derives* unique factorization in ℤ[ζ₂₃] from Lamé's hypotheses exposes the false premise directly. Effort: 2–4 weeks atop flt-regular. **Verdict: worth attacking** as the canonical demonstration that a century-famous gap is a single, formalizable false lemma.

### 4.2 Kempe's 1879 four-color "proof": the nine-vertex tangle

**The claim and the break.** Kempe's 1879 proof stood for eleven years, earned him a Royal Society fellowship, and broke on exactly one configuration: the pentagon case, where two Kempe-chain interchanges (a red–yellow chain and a red–green chain, say) cannot be performed independently because the chains interfere — the "irrevocable Kempe chain tangle," exhibited concretely on the nine-vertex Fritsch graph ([MacTutor](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/HistTopics/The_four_colour_theorem/), [Gethner et al., Involve 2009](https://msp.org/involve/2009/2-3/involve-v2-n3-p01-s.pdf), [Math StackExchange](https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/1687880/kempes-proof-of-the-four-colour-theorem)).

**The Lean slice and gate.** Graph colorings, planar maps via combinatorial maps, and chain-switching are all formalizable with current graph-theory libraries (the four color theorem itself was formalized in Coq by Gonthier). The pinpoint artifact has two halves: (i) formalize Kempe's inductive argument *with the pentagon step isolated as a lemma*; (ii) exhibit the Fritsch graph with its explicit pre-coloring and verify, by finite computation, that both switch orders produce a tangle — the lemma's hypothesis pattern holds while its conclusion fails. **Gate values:** on the Fritsch graph, one switch order 4-colors successfully and the other irrevocably tangles — a signed, two-bit computation that constitutes the formal "the error is here" ([Gethner et al.](https://msp.org/involve/2009/2-3/involve-v2-n3-p01-s.pdf)). Effort: 4–8 weeks (planarity is the expensive part; the tangle itself is days). **Verdict: worth attacking**; this is the highest pedagogical-yield-per-week item in the report, and it builds the Kempe-chain machinery any future elementary 4CT attempt would be audited against.

### 4.3 Tait's conjecture (1884) and the Tutte graph (1946): a 46-vertex refutation object

**The claim and the break.** Tait conjectured that every 3-connected planar cubic graph is Hamiltonian — had it held, the four color theorem would have followed. Tutte killed it in 1946 with a 46-vertex graph assembled from three copies of the "Tutte fragment," whose forced-path property makes a Hamiltonian cycle combinatorially impossible; the smallest counterexamples have 38 vertices (Holton–McKay, 1988) ([Wikipedia: Tait's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tait%27s_conjecture), [Wikipedia: Tutte graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutte_graph), [MathWorld](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/TaitsHamiltonianGraphConjecture.html)).

**The Lean slice and gate.** This is a *finite negative certificate*: "the Tutte graph has no Hamiltonian cycle" is a decidable proposition about 46 vertices, provable by verified exhaustive search or, more elegantly, by formalizing the fragment argument (no Hamiltonian path with endpoints a,b in the fragment — itself a tiny exhaustive check ([Fleischner et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.08310))). **Gate values:** fragment path enumeration (confirm: exactly the claimed (a,c)/(b,c) path inventories), then the three-way junction contradiction. Effort: 2–4 weeks. **Verdict: worth attacking**, bundling with §4.2 into one "planar/cubic graph certificate" library.

### 4.4 Pólya and Mertens: conjectures that computation killed — the trust anchors

**Pólya (1919).** L(x) = Σ_{n≤x} λ(n) ≤ 0 for all x ≥ 2, which would have implied RH and the simplicity of all zeta zeros, was disproved by Haselgrove in 1958 with a counterexample estimated near 1.845×10³⁶¹; Lehman gave the explicit L(906,180,359) = +1 in 1960, and Tanaka found the smallest counterexample, L(906,150,257) = +1, in 1980 ([Wikipedia: Pólya conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%B3lya_conjecture), [Brent, CWI](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/19722/19722D.pdf), [Humphries survey](https://arxiv.org/html/1108.1524v2)). **Mertens (1897).** |M(x)| < √x was disproved by Odlyzko and te Riele in 1985 via lattice basis reduction — and, remarkably, no disproof avoiding lattice reduction is known to this day ([arXiv:2502.21021](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21021v1)).

**The Lean slice and gate.** These are not fragile proofs to refute but **numeric counterexample certificates** to canonize. For Pólya: a verified computation of L(906,150,257) = +1 with a formally checked sieve (checkpointed prefix sums of λ, each chunk a compact certificate) — a heavy but bounded computation, feasible as a distributed/verified artifact. For Mertens: the Odlyzko–te Riele disproof reduces to exhibiting a short vector in an explicit lattice whose existence forces M(x)/√x to exceed 1 somewhere; the gate is the lattice reduction certificate itself (LLL-style, formally checkable), plus routine complex-analytic lemmas. **Verdict: Pólya — worth attacking (4–8 weeks, mostly engineering); Mertens — partial slice only** (the analytic number theory around the explicit formula is the bottleneck; budget it as a medium project, not weeks). Both serve as the genre's permanent warning: small-case evidence is not a proof, and here is the machine-checked corpse to prove it.

## 5. Tier 3: The Mega-Claims — Why They Fail the Rubric

**Zhang's Landau–Siegel zeros preprint (2022).** *Discrete mean estimates and the Landau–Siegel zero* (arXiv:2211.02515) claims results that would severely constrain or exclude Siegel zeros; Zhang's related 2007 attempt had known problems and was never published, and the 2022 paper's public refereeing status remains unresolved ([Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2022-11-theorist-solution-landau-siegel-zeros-conjecture.html), [Tao's blog](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2021/09/15/the-hardy-littlewood-chowla-conjecture-in-the-presence-of-a-siegel-zero/)). The decisive content is a tower of discrete mean-value estimates with delicate exponent bookkeeping — superficially "just inequalities," but resting on deep sieve machinery with no mathlib substrate. Formalizability ≈ 4: a *slice* (the final exponent comparison at the paper's claimed parameter choices) could be extracted and numerically spot-checked, but isolating it requires expert navigation of ~100 pages of estimates. **Verdict: partial slice only — high effort, medium yield; revisit if a claimed elementary re-proof appears.**

**Joshi's abc (2024–25) and the IUT aftermath.** Joshi's *Construction of Arithmetic Teichmüller Spaces IV: A proof of the abc-conjecture* (arXiv:2403.10430) claims to repair the Scholze–Stix objection to Mochizuki's Corollary 3.12; it is rejected by Mochizuki's camp ("no meaningful mathematical content whatsoever") and has not persuaded the critics either, amid a controversy now carrying a prize of up to $1M ([International Maths Challenge report](https://international-maths-challenge.com/mathematicians-are-bitterly-divided-over-a-controversial-proof/), [Ramsundar](https://deepforest.substack.com/p/tilting-at-mathematical-windmills), [Joshi's Final Report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.10568)). The Scholze–Stix objection itself — the comparison of two mathematical objects in Corollary 3.12 — is as close to a "pinpointed broken step" as this saga offers, but the objects involved (Θ-links, Hodge theaters) have no plausible mathlib encoding on any weeks- or even years-scale budget. **Verdict: do not attack.** Note for the record that Scholze's *own* checkpoint of a different theorem — the Liquid Tensor Experiment — succeeded because the statement, though deep, was formalizable; abc/IUT fails that test at the statement level.

**de Branges' RH claims (2004–).** De Branges — the solver of Bieberbach, working outside his specialty, the genre's textbook flag — announced a 124-page RH proof in June 2004 and has revised it for two decades; the community consensus is that no checkable proof is present in the manuscripts ([MacTutor](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Branges/)). Fragility 9+, but there is no formalizable core and no finite gate: you cannot refute, in Lean, a proof whose decisive step does not exist as mathematics. **Verdict: do not attack.** File under "why the rubric exists": fragility without refutability is a tar pit.

**Calibration contrast.** The pattern is visible in the positive column: PFR closed in three weeks because its spine was entropy inequalities over finite groups ([arXiv:2311.05762](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.05762)); the cap set closed in weeks because its spine was polynomial-method linear algebra over finite fields ([ITP 2019](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITP.2019.15)); Keller closed end-to-end because its spine was a graph-clique reduction plus SAT certificates ([DROPS/LIPIcs ITP 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITP.2026.26)). The mega-claims fail precisely where these succeed.

## 6. The Calibration Gallery: Correct-but-Computational Proofs as Testbeds

Four verified successes deserve a scouting note because they define the *shape* a formalizable proof takes, and because each would have been indistinguishable from a fragile claim at preprint time had the certificates not been published.

The **q-TSPP proof** (Koutschan–Kauers–Zeilberger, PNAS 2011) reduced the Andrews–Robbins conjecture to a determinant evaluation and thence to three identities, each closed by a creative-telescoping certificate — the final one being a recurrence of order 12 whose leading coefficient never vanishes, so that checking the identity at n = 1,…,12 completes the proof ([arXiv:1002.4384](https://arxiv.org/html/1002.4384v2)). I spot-checked the product formula at q = 1: it yields the TSPP orbit counts 2, 5, 16, 66, 352, matching the known enumeration. The entire argument is "rational-function certificate + finite initial-value check" — the purest existing model of a Lean gate, and still awaiting a full formalization. **de Grey's 2018 chromatic-number-of-the-plane lower bound** rests on a 1,581-vertex unit-distance graph that a computer check showed is not 4-colorable ([Quanta](https://www.quantamagazine.org/decades-old-graph-problem-yields-to-amateur-mathematician-20180417/)); the non-4-colorability is a SAT certificate, while the *unit-distance embedding* (exact coordinates in a finite extension of ℚ) is the formalization-critical half — a genuinely instructive two-layer gate. The **Boolean Pythagorean triples** and **Keller d = 7** results were covered in §1; note their shared architecture — reduction lemma (human-sized) + UNSAT certificate (machine-sized) + formally verified checker — which is now the reference template, culminating in the 2026 end-to-end Lean 4 Keller verification ([Cruz-Filipe & Schneider-Kamp](http://imada.sdu.dk/~lcf/pubs/paper37.pdf), [DROPS/LIPIcs ITP 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITP.2026.26)).

Two further genre anchors deserve mention. **Helfgott's ternary Goldbach proof** couples analytic number theory with Platt's interval-arithmetic verification of both the small cases (every odd number in [7, 8.875·10³⁰] is a sum of three primes) and a massive finite RH check for Dirichlet L-functions — a correct, non-fragile proof whose computational gates are nonetheless exactly the kind of object a Lean interval-arithmetic library could re-verify ([arXiv:1404.2224](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.2224), [Ramaré's sketch](https://ramare-olivier.github.io/Maths/Helfgott-Ramare-2.pdf)). And **Opfer's 2011 Collatz claim** — a Hamburg numerical analyst stepping outside his specialty, submitting to *Mathematics of Computation*, then withdrawing when the flaw surfaced — remains the cleanest modern specimen of the fragility signature, though Collatz claims offer no finite gate and are excluded from the target list on refutability grounds ([Lipton's blog](https://rjlipton.com/2011/06/06/types-of-papers/), [mathsbyagirl](https://mathsbyagirl.wordpress.com/2016/02/29/collatz-conjecture/)).

## 8. Attack Plans for the Top Three

**Target 1 — the 2026 odd-zeta preprint.** Week 1: extract the paper's explicit formulas for A_m^(q), B_m^(q) and the claimed parameter ranges; implement in exact rational + interval arithmetic; compute ‖Λ_m^(q)‖ for ζ(5) at m ≤ 50. Three outcomes: decay faster than e^{−5} per step (escalate — possible breakthrough), failure to decay or identically-zero forms (write the refutation note), or formulas too under-specified to compute (itself a reportable fragility finding: "asymptotic claims asserted without displayed computation"). Weeks 2–6: formalize the general irrationality criterion and the linear-form construction in Lean 4 against mathlib's zeta API, reusing the Apéry formalization's denominator machinery ([arXiv:2503.07625](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07625v1)), leaving the decay estimate as the single gated hypothesis. Deliverable either way: a public Lean artifact + a note.

**Target 2 — the Erdős–Straus congruence system.** Days 1–3: transcribe the claimed covering families into an executable checker. Week 1 gate: run all primes < 10⁵ in the six hard classes mod 840 (there are ~1,600 of them); log the first uncovered prime or the first parameter violation of the claimed divisibility (e.g., the paper's "automatic" odd-k case — I note its proof rests on "taking d=u=1, v=2," a three-constant fold that deserves exact scrutiny) ([arXiv:2404.01508](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01508v3)). Weeks 2–4: formalize the divisor-parametrization lemma (a/b is 2-Egyptian iff ∃ u,v | b with a | u+v) and the reduction-to-primes chain in Lean; state the covering claim as a theorem and either prove it or attach the counterexample prime. The infrastructure is reusable against the steady drip of future Erdős–Straus claims, of which the literature shows no shortage ([arXiv:2101.00975](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.00975v1), [arXiv:2508.07367](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07367v1)).

**Target 3 — the Agoh–Giuga genre kit.** One-time build (3–4 weeks): mathlib-quality formalizations of Korselt's criterion, Giuga's criterion (n composite counterexample ⟺ Giuga ∩ Carmichael), von Staudt–Clausen, and the Agoh–Bernoulli equivalence — all elementary, all missing or partial in current libraries. Per claim thereafter: blueprint the claimed proof against this kit; the historical base rate says the failure will be a divisibility assertion that the kit can instantiate at g = 30 or g = 858 to produce a false instance. Secondary deliverable: a verified record of the seven known Giuga numbers and their non-Carmichael status (my spot-check above is the prototype), which is the standing counterexample-oracle for the genre ([CARMA survey](https://carmamaths.org/jon/giuga2013.pdf), [ProofAtlas](https://proofatlas.ai/collaboration/agoh-giuga-conjecture/)).

## 9. Caveats and Conduct

Three caveats govern the use of this list. First, **fragility is not guilt**: the de Grey graph and the q-TSPP certificates had the same surface profile as fragile claims at announcement — unorthodox authors, computational decisive steps — and both are correct ([Quanta](https://www.quantamagazine.org/decades-old-graph-problem-yields-to-amateur-mathematician-20180417/), [arXiv:1002.4384](https://arxiv.org/html/1002.4384v2)). The gate is an instrument for finding out, not a verdict in advance. Second, **refuting a route is not refuting a theorem**: ζ(5) is very probably irrational, FLT is true, and the four color theorem is true; what Lean can kill cheaply is a specific lemma chain, and the public write-up should say exactly that. Third, several live targets involve named individuals and unresolved community processes (Zhang, Joshi, the 2026 preprints); the correct posture is the one modeled by Chen et al.'s ζ(5) note — dispassionate, equation-number-specific, and addressed to the mathematics ([arXiv:2411.16774](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16774v3)). A refutation artifact should read like a bug report, not an indictment: here is the lemma, here is the instance, here is the false instance.

Finally, a note on the numbers themselves. Scores in §7 are calibrated judgments, not measurements; the effort estimates assume one expert formalizer with current mathlib and standard blueprint tooling. The September 2026 FLT autoformalization — eleven days, largely autonomous, kernel-checked — suggests those estimates may soon look conservative, and that the per-claim audit loop proposed in §8 could eventually run at the speed at which claims are posted ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formalizing-fermats-last-theorem)).

*This report is a research-scouting document for mathematical verification planning; scores and assessments are the author's analytical judgment, not statements of fact about any claimant's conduct or intent.*
